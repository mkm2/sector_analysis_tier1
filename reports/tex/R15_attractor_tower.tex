\begin{abstract}
In the right-hand panel of R9's F1 a column of V+reset rules stands at
$a_\att=1$ with $b_\att$ running from $1.41$ to $1.89$: a bounded number of
attractors, the largest of which still grows exponentially. There are $68$ such
rules of the $160$ V+reset ones. Their growth bases are not spread out --- $61$
of the $68$ sit on five \emph{exact} algebraic constants, $\varphi$ and $\sqrt3$
taking $21$ each. The natural explanation is that the dissipative channels never
act inside the largest attractor, so what grows there is really the underlying
unitary. \textbf{That is true for exactly $12$ of the $68$, and false for the
other $56$.} For the twelve it is true in the strongest sense: the largest
attractor \emph{is} the largest sector of the coherent parent, state for state,
and all twelve sit at $\varphi$. For the other fifty-six the resets do work at
$\Theta(N)$ sites --- the count of firing sites grows linearly with system size,
so this is not a boundary effect --- and the exponential growth survives
dissipation rather than avoiding it.

\textbf{The mechanism common to both} is a subshift. The largest attractor is a
subshift of finite type over the two-site brick-wall unit cell: membership is
decided by which pairs of adjacent $2$-site blocks occur, and
$b_\att=\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}(T)}$ for the $4\times4$ block-transition matrix
$T$. Verified exactly --- the grammar learned from the attractor regenerates the
attractor and nothing else --- for $62$ of the $68$ at two sizes, plus one more
with an extra block of memory. A reset is a \emph{local} map, so the set it
leaves invariant is locally constrained, so it is counted by a transfer matrix
whatever the map does to individual states; unitarity is sufficient for an
algebraic base, not necessary. The five block Perron roots are
$2$, $1+\sqrt2$, $\varphi^2$, $3$, $2+\sqrt2$ and $(3+\sqrt{17})/2$, all roots
of quadratics. This also names a constant the numerics had left open,
$\sqrt{1+\sqrt2}=1.5537740$, and leaves exactly one unidentified: the $1.718005$
of rules $169$ and $225$, now bounded above by $\sqrt3$. Nine rules reach
$\varphi$ \emph{with} firing resets, so the constant does not determine which
of the two mechanisms is at work. \rt{obc0}, $N=10$ for the census.
\end{abstract}

\section{The tower}
\label{sec:tower}

A rule is in the tower if it is V+reset, its attractor \emph{count} is flat
($a_\att=1$ to within $0.02$) and its largest attractor grows exponentially
($b_\att>1.02$). That is $68$ rules.

\subsection{Where the bases sit}

\begin{center}
\iftable{tab_r15_bases_obc0.tex}
\end{center}

\noindent Five constants absorb $61$ of the $68$, and for those the base comes
from an exact integer recurrence rather than a fit:
$\sqrt2$ ($3$ rules), $\varphi$ ($21$), $\sqrt3$ ($20$ of $21$),
$2\cos(\pi/8)=\sqrt{2+\sqrt2}$ ($8$) and $\sqrt{(3+\sqrt{17})/2}$ ($9$). The
remaining seven are fits. Two of them, at $1.847632$, are within
$1.3\times10^{-4}$ of $2\cos(\pi/8)$ and are almost certainly that constant
imperfectly fitted; the pair at $1.5537/1.5539$ and the pair at $1.7180$ have no
identification here and are left as fits rather than rounded into a name.

These are the constants of \emph{constrained binary words} --- Perron roots of
small transfer matrices --- which is exactly the company the unitary rules keep.
That is what makes the idle-reset hypothesis so natural.

\section{The hypothesis, tested}

\begin{quote}
\emph{The dissipative channels never fire on the largest attractor, so what
grows there is the underlying unitary.}
\end{quote}

\noindent A reset \rt{D} writes $0$ and \rt{E} writes $1$; either does
\emph{work} only when the target bit is not already at that value, and
otherwise acts as the identity on that state. \fn{tower.firing\_sites} runs the
brick-wall sweep by hand, following every Hadamard branch, and returns the sites
at which some branch had a reset do work. (The hand sweep is validated against
the engine's successor map in the tests: it must reproduce \fn{wcc.make\_succ}
exactly, over all states of $37$ rules at three sizes, or the firing flags mean
nothing.)

\midrule
no reset ever fires on the largest attractor & $\mathbf{12}$ & hypothesis holds \\
some reset fires & $\mathbf{56}$ & hypothesis fails \\

\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{The twelve, and why they work}

\begin{center}
\iftable{tab_r15_idle_obc0.tex}
\end{center}

\noindent For all twelve the statement holds in the strongest available form:
the child's successor map \emph{coincides} with the coherent parent's on every
state of the largest attractor, and the attractor has exactly the size of the
parent's largest sector. It is not merely that the resets happen to be idle ---
the attractor \emph{is} a sector of the parent, state for state.

They fall into two families of six, and the mechanism is visible in the tuple:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{llll}
\toprule
family & tuples & parent & $|A|$ at $N=10$ \\
\midrule
\rt{V} at $r_{00}$, rest from $\{$\rt{D},\rt{I}$\}$ &
  \rt{VDDD}, \rt{VIDD}, \rt{VDID}, \rt{VDDI}, \rt{VIDI}, \rt{VDII} &
  $W201$ & $144=F_{12}$ \\
\rt{V} at $r_{11}$, rest from $\{$\rt{E},\rt{I}$\}$ &
  \rt{IEIV}, \rt{EEIV}, \rt{IIEV}, \rt{EIEV}, \rt{IEEV}, \rt{EEEV} &
  $W108$ & $55=F_{10}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent The reset's target value agrees with what those sites already carry.
In the first family the Hadamard fires only on the neighbourhood $00$, and every
\rt{D} sits at a neighbourhood that occurs only when the site is already $0$, so
writing $0$ changes nothing. The second family is the same statement with
$0\leftrightarrow1$ and \rt{E} for \rt{D}. The surviving set is the parent's
Fibonacci-counted sector, hence $\varphi$ --- and indeed \textbf{every one of the
twelve sits at $\varphi$}, none at any other constant.

\subsection{The fifty-six: the resets fire throughout}

For these the resets fire, and not marginally: the number of \emph{sites} at
which some reset does work on the largest attractor grows linearly with $N$
(right panel of the figure), typically reaching $N-1$ or $N$. So it cannot be
rescued as a boundary effect or as a bounded set of defect sites --- dissipation
acts throughout the bulk of the attractor, and the exponential growth survives
it rather than avoiding it.

\subsection{So what \emph{is} the mechanism? A subshift on the unit cell}
\label{sec:sft}

The attractor is a \textbf{subshift of finite type over the two-site brick-wall
unit cell}. Write a state as $N/2$ blocks of two sites. Then membership in the
largest attractor is decided entirely by \emph{which pairs of adjacent blocks
occur} --- nothing longer-range is needed --- and the growth base is
\[
b_\att \;=\; \sqrt{\lambda_{\max}(T)},
\]
with $T$ the $4\times4$ zero-one matrix of allowed block-to-block transitions.
The square root is because one block is two sites.

This is checked, not assumed. \fn{tower.sft\_check} learns the grammar
\emph{from} the attractor --- the first blocks, the allowed adjacent pairs, the
last blocks --- regenerates every state the grammar permits, and asks whether
that reproduces the attractor exactly. It does for \textbf{62 of the 68} at
$N=10$ and $N=12$ simultaneously, and one more (rule $118$) with a single extra
block of memory. For all of them $\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}}$ matches the reported
base, with no exceptions.

\begin{tabular}{rlrrl}

\midrule

\paragraph{The grammars, explicitly.} Writing a block as its two site values
and indexing $T$ by $00,10,01,11$:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{rlrll}
\toprule
rule & $b_\att$ & allowed pairs & blocks used & the constraint \\
\midrule
$3$   & $\sqrt3$ & $9/16$ & $00,10,01$ & \textbf{the block $11$ never occurs; the other three are free} \\
$11$  & $\varphi$ & $8/16$ & $00,10,01$ & as above, and $00$ cannot be followed by $10$ \\
$191$ & $\sqrt2$ & $6/16$ & $10,01,11$ & $00$ never occurs, and only two successors survive \\
$167$ & $\sqrt{1+\sqrt2}$ & $10/16$ & all four & \\
$35$  & $2\cos(\pi/8)$ & $14/16$ & all four & only $11\to10$ and $11\to11$ forbidden \\
$19$  & $\sqrt{(3+\sqrt{17})/2}$ & $14/16$ & all four & only $11\to00$ and $11\to01$ forbidden \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent Rule $3$ is the cleanest and it covers the whole $\sqrt3$ group of
$19$: the surviving states are exactly those in which \emph{the block $11$ never
appears}, with no further condition, so the attractor is all words over a
three-letter block alphabet and $|A|=3^{N/2}$ exactly --- $729$ at $N=12$, as
computed. At the other extreme rules $35$ and $19$ forbid only two of the
sixteen block pairs each, and the difference between which two is the whole
difference between $2\cos(\pi/8)$ and $\sqrt{(3+\sqrt{17})/2}$.

\noindent \textbf{This is why the firing resets do not spoil anything.} A reset
is a \emph{local} map, so the set it leaves invariant is cut out by local
constraints, and a locally-constrained set of words is counted by a transfer
matrix regardless of what the map does to individual states. Unitarity on the
attractor is \emph{sufficient} for an algebraic base --- that is the twelve ---
but it is not necessary. Locality is what does the work, and every rule in the
tower is local by construction.

Seen this way the two mechanisms are one mechanism at different strengths. The
twelve inherit the parent's transfer matrix outright because the resets write
nothing; the fifty-six get a \emph{different} transfer matrix, because the
resets forbid block pairs the parent allowed. Both are subshifts on the same
unit cell.

than exact recurrences, four are now pinned:

\subsection{Five rules that are not subshifts at this order}

Rules $97$, $117$, $123$, $169$ and $225$ are not SFTs on blocks at order $1$ or
$2$: the learned grammar regenerates \emph{more} than the attractor. Since the
grammar is learned from the attractor, the closure always contains it, so
$\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}}$ is an \emph{upper bound} on the true base and the
attractor is a proper subshift of it --- sofic, or of longer range. Rules $169$
and $225$ are the sharpest case: closure base $\sqrt3=1.732051$, measured
$1.718005$. That pair remains the one genuinely unidentified constant in the
tower, now with a bound rather than nothing.

\subsection{The constant does not identify the mechanism}

Twenty-one rules sit at $\varphi$; twelve are idle and \textbf{nine are not}
--- rules $11$, $47$, $81$, $117$, $139$, $161$, $171$, $209$ and $241$ reach
the same golden ratio with resets firing at $\Theta(N)$ sites. So one cannot
read the mechanism off the growth base, which is worth saying because the
temptation to do so is exactly what this report set out to test.

\section{What this settles and what it does not}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Settled.} The idle-reset explanation is a real mechanism and it is
  a minority one: $12$ of $68$, all at $\varphi$, all inheriting a parent sector
  outright.
\item \textbf{Settled.} For the other $56$ the resets act on $\Theta(N)$ sites of
  the attractor, so no version of ``the channel is secretly unitary there''
  survives.
\item \textbf{Settled} (\S\ref{sec:sft}). The transfer matrices, for $63$ of
  the $68$: the attractor is a subshift on the $2$-site unit cell and
  $b_\att=\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}(T)}$, with the five Perron roots all quadratic
  irrationals. This is the mechanism for the firing rules and it subsumes the
  idle ones.
\item \textbf{Settled.} One of the two unidentified pairs:
  $1.5537$ is $\sqrt{1+\sqrt2}$.
\item \textbf{Open.} The $1.7180$ pair (rules $169$, $225$), which is not a
  subshift at order $1$ or $2$ on blocks. Its SFT closure gives the bound
  $b_\att<\sqrt3$, and the true value is $1.718005$.
\item \textbf{Open.} Rules $97$, $117$, $123$ likewise fail the subshift test
  at this order; their attractors are sofic or longer-range.
\item \textbf{Caveat.} \rt{obc0}, and the census is at $N=10$ with the
  firing-site scaling checked at $N=8,10,12$. Attractors are monitored-only
  objects, so everything here is a statement about the measured chain.
\end{itemize}
