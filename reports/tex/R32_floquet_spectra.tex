\begin{abstract}
We diagonalise the one-period Floquet operator $U$ of all sixteen unitary rules with the Hadamard gate, for up to $N=14$ to $20$ sites with open boundaries (the maximum depends on the rule's sector sizes) and
$N=6\ldots14$ on rings. For every eigenstate we record the quasienergy, the half-chain entanglement and the gap ratio.
All symmetries are resolved first: Krylov sectors, the blocks of the algebra generated by the local gates (which also
captures quantum fragmentation and hidden internal symmetries), and the spatial symmetry. The spatial symmetry is the
reflection for odd $N$ and, for even $N$, the half step $\Omega=RL_A$ with $\Omega^2=U$.

Because $U$ is real orthogonal and a product of two reflections, the chaotic reference is the $\beta=1$ value
$\langle r\rangle=0.5307$, computed on the half spectrum. We verified this against random reflections.

The sixteen rules sort by the number $k$ of neighbour patterns that fire the gate:
\begin{itemize}
\item $k=3$ (54, 57, 99, 147): the only chaotic rules on the open chain. At $N=14$, $\langle r\rangle=0.531$ for 57 and
  99, and $0.527$ for 147, the latter only after symmetry resolution (0.422 without). Each carries an $N$-independent number of exact eigenstates of
  every individual gate: two or four product states, and for 54 a single entangled area-law state.
\item $k=1$: exponentially fragmented, with Poisson statistics inside, as expected for tensor products of independent rooms.
\item $k=2$ XOR (150, 105): arithmetic spectra. For 150 the count of distinct quasienergies reproduces R8's $3^m$ law
  exactly up to $N=14$.
\item $k=2$ one-sided: a boundary-driven block tower, with $\langle r\rangle$ near Poisson but drifting upward.
  \emph{On a ring the tower disappears and these rules are chaotic} ($\beta=1$ in the shape of $P(r)$, although they have
  no mirror symmetry). This class is a boundary phenomenon.
\item $k=0,4$: trivial.
\end{itemize}
On rings (Sec.~\ref{sec:pbc}), the $k=3$, XOR, $k=1$ and trivial classes survive. The ensemble of the chaotic rules then
follows their symmetry: 57 and 99 (no mirror) become CUE with $\langle r\rangle=0.602$ in complex momentum sectors, and
147 is COE.

With the X gate (Sec.~\ref{sec:x}) every rule is a classical reversible automaton. 54 and 147 are then the integrable
RCA54, and only 57/99 have orbits that fill a finite fraction of the space, and only on the open chain. Quantum chaos
with $H$ therefore does not require classical chaos with $X$.
The literature on these diagnostics for Floquet operators is reviewed, with every reference checked online.
\end{abstract}
